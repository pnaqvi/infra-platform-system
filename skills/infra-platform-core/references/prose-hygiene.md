# Prose Hygiene — Shareable Documents (T12)

*Standalone distillation of the be-human catalog (itself adapted from Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing"). Where the full be-human skill is installed, it governs and this file is the fallback; the patterns are the same.*

*A compact scrub list for anything Parvez sends: reviews, plans, memos, one-pagers, emails. Applied at drafting and again before `final`. The tell is density and reflexiveness — any single item is fine in isolation; the same move on autopilot is not. If a sentence says the same thing with the flourish removed, remove it.*

1. **Inflated significance.** Cut *pivotal, crucial, vital role, underscores, testament to, watershed, lasting impact*. State what happened with a number; let the reader judge the weight. "Adoption reached 62% against a 60% target" beats "a significant milestone underscoring strong momentum."
2. **Promotional tone.** Cut *world-class, robust, seamless, cutting-edge, best-in-class, boasts*. A status document tells; it does not sell — reds included.
3. **Editorializing asides.** Cut *it's worth noting, importantly, notably, interestingly*. If it's worth noting, note it; the noticing is shown by inclusion.
4. **The rule of three.** "Faster, cheaper, and safer" three times a page reads as machine cadence. Vary structure; one precise claim beats three vague ones.
5. **Negative parallelisms.** "This isn't just X — it's Y" is filler shape. Say Y.
6. **Filler transitions.** *Moreover, furthermore, additionally, in conclusion* at paragraph heads — cut; order the content so it doesn't need connective tissue.
7. **Reflexive summaries.** Don't end sections by restating them. The headline assessment at the top is the one permitted summary.
8. **Trailing "-ing" clauses.** "…slipped two weeks, impacting the Q4 milestone and requiring replanning" — break into sentences with subjects and verbs.
9. **Weasel attributions.** *Industry experts agree, it is widely recognized* — name the source or drop the claim (T2 already demands this).
10. **Formatting overkill.** Bold sparingly (decisions, owners, dates). No bullet where a sentence serves; no nested bullets in an exec document. Tables for numbers, prose for judgment.
11. **Chatbot residue.** No "certainly," "great question," "I hope this helps," or meta-commentary about the document in the document.
12. **Hedging stacks.** One qualifier per claim maximum. "May potentially help mitigate" → "reduces, at medium confidence."

The positive form: short declarative sentences, numbers over adjectives, active voice, owners and dates attached to every commitment, reds stated as plainly as greens.
