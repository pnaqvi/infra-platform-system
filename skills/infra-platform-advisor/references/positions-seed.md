# Positions Seeding Interview — Platform Program

*Run when the memory sections are empty. ~20 minutes, permanent payback. For each domain, elicit: his position (one sentence), confidence (high/medium/low), basis, and the observable that would change his mind. Write results via infra-platform-memory into the named sections. Skip domains he defers; record "no position yet" is itself allowed.*

## program-strategy
1. What does "winning" look like for this program in 3 years — and what will you explicitly NOT do (workloads, domains, internal customers who get "no" or "later")?
2. What is the honest diagnosis — the 2–3 root problems this platform must solve that the current model cannot?
3. What is your MVP conviction: which single high-value use case proves the platform first, and why that one?
4. Phase gates: what observable results must exist before the program scales beyond MVP?

## architecture
5. Anchor posture per domain — compute, container, network, storage, observability, data services: one primary technology each, or where do you deliberately run two, and why?
6. Build-vs-buy line: what will this program custom-build as a true differentiator, and what is bought/consumed no matter how tempting the build?
7. Lock-in tolerance: which anchor dependencies are acceptable one-way doors, and what mitigation must exist in writing?
8. Everything-as-code posture: where must policy/config/pipeline/infra-as-code be non-negotiable from day one?

## platform-products
9. Product taxonomy: what are the first 3–5 platform products, and who is the named customer persona for each?
10. Adoption thesis: for each early product, why will teams choose it over what they do today — the honest answer, not the pitch?

## vendors
11. Standing vendor beliefs: which vendor claims in this space do you distrust by default, and which partners have earned trust?

## org-talent (org-level only)
12. Team model conviction: how do you want IPE teams composed and sized, and what will you *not* copy from the current org?

## metrics-value
13. The 4–5 metrics you'd stake the program's credibility on in year one — and the one metric you refuse to be managed by.

Close by proposing each answer as a position entry (with confidence and would-change-my-mind), confirm, write, commit.
